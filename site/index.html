The goal

You start beside the Furnivall Sculling Club pontoon (Middlesex bank, just above Hammersmith Bridge) and row where you like, among other crews. Rowing as far as the UL Boat House – past Hammersmith, Chiswick Eyot, Barnes and Chiswick bridges – is optional and earns a bonus.

When you are back at the pontoon (the green and gold ring, on the Middlesex side) after rowing away from it, a Get off the boat button appears. Press it to finish: only then is your row saved to the boards. Exit, under the little map, takes you back to this menu without saving (tap it twice).

Steering and the lines

The boat never steers itself: you have to hold Steer left or Steer right to turn the bow. It then keeps its heading, so in a bend you must keep turning to stay in the river, and you can run aground or hit piers, pontoons, bridge piers and other crews.

Three dashed lines run along the river: the left line, the middle line and the right line. They show where each lane lies. The line you are following correctly (within a few metres of it, pointing the right way) turns yellow and thick; the others stay faint white. The Tideway Code panel tells you which line to take.

Lanes, tide and low water

Left and right always mean left and right in your direction of travel. The left and right lines are the Inshore Zones along each bank; the middle line is the starboard (right-hand) half of the Fairway, the deeper channel in the middle.

The tide comes live from the Environment Agency tide gauges. At low tide the river is narrower, especially on the inside of bends, and an Inshore Zone can dry out: its line then moves in to the edge of the water, and if you stray into the shallows you run aground. The stream is strongest in the deep water, on the outside of bends, and weakest near the banks.

The Tideway Code

The game follows the PLA's Tideway Code for the Upper Tideway.

  • Against the stream (flood going up river, ebb going down): work the slacks in the Inshore Zone on the slack bank – the Surrey bank from Hammersmith to the Chiswick Steps Crossing Zone, then the Middlesex bank up to the UL Boat House – and cross only inside the yellow Crossing Zones.
  • With the stream, or at slack water: keep to the starboard side of the Fairway.
  • Out of position for more than 4 seconds costs a Tideway Code penalty: +5 s and −50 points.
  • Hitting a bridge pier or a pontoon, or running aground, costs +15 s and −150 points.
  • Bridge arches are numbered #1 (Middlesex side) to #3 (Surrey side), and the boat aims for the middle of an arch. Bridge piers and pontoons are obstacles.

Turning round

Press U to sweep across the river and head back. The Code says never to spin on the spot, so the boat makes a wide arc by itself while it turns; take over the steering again once it has come round. It needs room, and the tide carries you while you turn, so don't turn just upstream of a bridge or pier. Past the end of the map the boat turns round by itself.

Other crews and overtaking

Single sculls to eights share the river, some sitting still and some rowing slowly. They always obey the Code: stationary boats and boats going against the stream sit in the Inshore Zone, and boats going with the stream keep to the starboard side of the Fairway.

  • Clean overtake (+100): pass on the outside (the river-centre side), with room to spare, never within 75 m of a bridge and never into oncoming traffic.
  • Dodgy overtake (−100 points, +5 s): anything else.
  • Hitting a boat: +15 s and −150 points.

You steer round other crews yourself. A warning appears when something is in your way, and you can use Space (easy oar) to hold back behind a slower boat near a bridge.

The coach

Break the Code, collide, run aground or overtake badly and the coach's launch roars up alongside to shout at you. It goes away once you behave.

Score

  • Distance rowed: +0.5 per metre
  • UL Boat House reached: +1000
  • Clean overtakes: +100 each · dodgy overtakes: −100 each
  • Tideway Code penalties: −50 each
  • Collisions and groundings: −150 each
  • Time: −1 per 10 seconds (the time includes the penalty seconds above)

The score is never below 0, and higher is better.

Crazyboard ranks the wildest rows by Chaos = 10 × collisions + 8 × groundings + 6 × dodgy overtakes + 3 × Code penalties.

River, bridges, buoys and landmarks – OpenStreetMap

The shape of the river, the banks, Chiswick Eyot, the bridges, the PLA Fairway buoys, the piers and pontoons and the club buildings come from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, used under the Open Database Licence (ODbL) (copyright and licence). Data was downloaded with the Overpass API. The main objects used:

  • River Thames water area (river banks)
  • Bridges: Hammersmith, Barnes, Chiswick
  • Furnivall Sculling Club and its pontoon
  • University of London Boat Club (the UL Boat House)
  • Chiswick Eyot

Piers, pontoons and buoys are mapped objects tagged man_made=pier and PLA buoys; there are too many to list one by one.

Tide – Environment Agency

The live water level comes from the Environment Agency real-time flood-monitoring API (tide gauges), © Environment Agency, published under the Open Government Licence v3.0. The game tries the Hammersmith gauge first, then the Richmond gauge (its tide runs about 30 minutes later), and a simulated tide if neither answers. The last 48 hours of readings give the low and high water marks; the stream speed is estimated from how fast the level is rising or falling.

Rules – PLA Tideway Code

The positioning, crossing, overtaking and turning rules come from the Port of London Authority's Tideway Code (PDF, November 2025 edition). The game is a simplified training version of the Code; the PLA text is the only authority.

What is estimated or invented

  • Exact bridge pier positions (they are not in OpenStreetMap) and the number of arches
  • The extent of the Crossing Zones and which bank is the slack bank: read from the Code's text, not from its zone maps
  • How much narrower the river gets at low tide, and the stream speeds across the channel
  • Boat speeds and handling, the other crews and the coach

Not for navigation. This is a game, not a guide to the river.
